Benchmark Protocol

Results are only comparable when everyone follows the same rules. Full details are in the paper and the benchmark README.

Re-identification

  • Four scenarios: reappearance, multi-camera, multi-camera long-term and long-term.
  • Each training subset is the gallery and the matching test subset is the query.
  • Subsets train_0 and test_0 are reserved for tuning and excluded from evaluation.
  • Report the average over all remaining subsets of each scenario.
  • Closed-set: distractors (negative IDs) are removed from the test sets. Open-set: distractors are kept.
  • Report the input size, pretraining data and whether CHIRLA data was used for training.

Tracking

  • Tune on the two training sequences only; evaluate on the eight test sequences (56 videos).
  • Report MOTA, IDF1, MT, ML, FN, FP, IDSW and FM on the full test set.
  • For the two occlusion scenarios, match each ground-truth box to the prediction with the highest IoU (> 0.5) and report MOTA, IDF1 and IDSW.
  • Report the detector and any appearance model you use.

Submit a Result

Evaluated a method on CHIRLA? Add it to the leaderboard so others can compare against it and cite your work.

Option 1: submission form

  1. Run your method following the protocol above.
  2. Open the submission form on GitHub.
  3. Fill in the method, setup, results, and a link to your paper or code, plus its BibTeX.

We review each submission and add it, usually within a week.

Option 2: pull request

  1. Add your entry to docs/leaderboard/results.json, and your paper under papers.
  2. Run python tools/validate_results.py to check the format.
  3. Open a pull request; the same check runs automatically.
